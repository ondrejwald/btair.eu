## 1. WELCOME

### 3.0 Main editorial / project annotation (80–120 words)

> Bridging the A-I-R is a European cooperation project that explores, prototypes and shares sustainable models of artistic residencies — with a focus on the people and organisations who host them. We connect independent cultural organisations, artists and mentors across six countries: Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Italy, Spain and Germany. Between 2026 and 2030, twelve pilot residencies will test new formats built on care, fair conditions and balance between centre and periphery. This platform is the project's central digital hub and open archive: everything we research, write and learn — open calls, methodologies, manuals and reports from the field — is published here, free for anyone designing or hosting a residency in Europe.

## 3.1 RESIDENCIES — MODELS

### 7.0 Paragraph on residency model research

> Before testing anything in practice, we mapped existing residency practices across Europe. The research looked at how programmes are funded, how artists are selected, what infrastructure and conditions hosts can offer, and what role mentors and local cultural mediators play. On this basis, the project formulated ten model scenarios for residency practice — each reflecting different centre typologies and stakeholder needs, and each measured against shared values: contextual sensitivity, low-impact mobility, participatory structure, inclusion and transferability. Four of these models were selected for long-term testing through twelve pilot residencies hosted by the project partners.

### 8.0 PDF annotation: Residency Models — Research Report

> A comparative study of residency models across the partner countries and beyond, resulting in ten model scenarios for residency practice. It covers funding structures, selection procedures, infrastructure, mentoring and the criteria of accessibility, care and sustainability. A practical starting point for any organisation designing its own residency format.

## 3.3 RESIDENCIES — REPORTS

### 12.0 Section intro paragraph

> Every residency in the project is documented from three perspectives. Over four years we will publish up to thirty short reports: twelve from resident artists, six from host organisations and twelve from mentors. These are not formal evaluations — they are personal, candid reflections on what happened, what worked and what surprised them, sometimes taking the form of statements, drawings, metaphors or photographs. Together they build a living record of what residencies actually feel like, from the inside.

### 13.0 Artist Report — Residency #01 (template)

> **Title:** Artist Report — Residency #01
> **Annotation (2 sentences):** A first-person account of [X] weeks at [host organisation, place], working on [theme/medium]. Reflections on the rhythm of the residency, the local context and the collaborations that emerged along the way.
>
> **Suggested structure for the full text (1–2 pages):**
> 1. Where I arrived and what I brought with me (expectations, project idea)
> 2. What actually happened (process, encounters, turning points)
> 3. What the place and people gave the work (context, community, mediator)
> 4. What surprised me / what I would do differently
> 5. What I take with me

### 14.0 Organisation Report — Host #01 (template)

> **Title:** Organisation Report — Host #01
> **Annotation (2 sentences):** What we learned about hospitality, logistics and what hosting a residency costs in practice. A candid look at being a host — not just a venue.
>
> **Suggested structure for the full text (1–2 pages):**
> 1. Why we hosted and which model we tested
> 2. Preparation: space, budget, communication, open call
> 3. The residency in practice: care, daily rhythm, community anchoring
> 4. What it really cost (time, money, energy) and what it gave back
> 5. Recommendations for other hosts

### 15.0 Mentor Report — Residency #01 (template)

> **Title:** Mentor Report — Residency #01
> **Annotation (2 sentences):** Notes from accompanying an artist through a research residency: shifting roles, listening more than advising, and the value of a second pair of eyes. A reflection on where mentoring helps — and where it should stay out of the way.
>
> **Suggested structure for the full text (1–2 pages):**
> 1. How the mentoring relationship was set up (frequency, format, agreements)
> 2. The artist's process from the mentor's perspective
> 3. Moments where mentoring mattered — and its boundaries
> 4. What I learned as a mentor
> 5. One piece of advice for future mentors

### 16.0 Report series — unified template and collection schedule (internal note)

> **Recommendation:** use the three structures above as a single shared template (one page of prompts sent to each author within 2 weeks of the residency ending; texts due within 6 weeks; light edit + English translation; publish in the term following each residency). Target volume per the Grant Agreement: 12 residencies → 12 artist + 12 mentor reports, 6 host organisations → 6 organisation reports. The series also feeds deliverable D3.8 (4 brief reflections and case records) and the database of good practices (D4.3), so tag each report by model, country and theme when publishing.

## 4. RESEARCH

### 17.0 Section intro paragraph

> This is the project's open library. Every document produced inside Bridging the A-I-R — methodologies, manuals, strategic papers and policy materials — is published here in full, free to download and released for re-use under an open licence. Together they form a practical toolkit for anyone designing, hosting, mentoring or funding an artistic residency in Europe today.

### 18.0 Annotation: Sustainability Methodology

> A framework for running residencies in an environmentally and socially sustainable way. It covers travel and low-impact mobility, hospitality, waste, fair pay and community relations, and is built from real cases tested inside the project's twelve pilot residencies. Conceived as a working tool: principles you can adapt, not rules you must obey.

### 19.0 Annotation: Strategic Document

> The project's strategic outlook on the future of artistic residencies in Central and Southern Europe. Drawing on four years of research and practice, it identifies structural gaps, shared priorities and opportunities for the independent sector — and outlines a framework for supporting residency infrastructure beyond 2030.

### 20.0 Annotation: Manual for Residency Providers

> A practical, step-by-step guide for organisations hosting residencies — especially smaller and emerging providers. From preparing the space and budget to contracts, communication, care for the artist and final evaluation, it distils the experience of six partner organisations into one usable handbook, including guidance on working with the ART-IN-RES platforms.

### 21.0 Annotation: Manual for Mentors

> What does it mean to mentor a resident artist? This guide describes the mentor's role and its boundaries, and offers concrete techniques tested by mentors across the project's residencies — from first meeting to final reflection. Part of the project's adaptable mentoring framework for residency practice.

### 22.0 Annotation: Manual for Open Call Design

> How to write an open call that is clear, fair and attractive to the right artists. Built on the project's jointly approved selection criteria — prioritising geographic proximity, low-impact mobility, inclusion and artistic relevance — it includes templates, checklists and real examples from the partners' calls.

### 23.0 Annotation: PR Articles & Press

> Collected press materials, articles and media coverage of Bridging the A-I-R, updated throughout the project. A resource for journalists, researchers and partners — for press enquiries, contact our communication manager (see Contacts).
